The useful distinction

A citation marker is not verification.

This tool organizes review evidence. It does not search databases, authenticate documents, determine controlling law, evaluate scientific validity, or independently prove that a source supports a proposition. Those conclusions require retrieving and reading the source.

Local-first: entries remain in this browser unless you export them. Use sanitized text; do not enter privileged, confidential, restricted, sealed, or personally identifying information.
Review brief

What decision depends on this material?

Define the review boundary before cataloguing citations. Different contexts carry different burdens and require different reviewers.

Claim ledger

Separate propositions before checking sources.

One sentence can contain several claims with different evidence burdens. Add each proposition that a reader, court, regulator, client, or decision-maker could reasonably rely on.

Source register

Make every authority retrievable.

Record enough information for another reviewer to locate the same source. DOI, PMID, docket, regulation identifier, official URL, accession number, or archived copy are stronger anchors than a title alone.

Source · identifier/URL · source type

URLs and identifiers are recorded as text. This workbench does not open or validate them automatically.

Verification findings

What did the source actually establish?

Record the reviewer’s retrieval and reading decision. “Supports” should mean the source was retrieved and the relevant passage, holding, dataset, or result was examined in context.

EVIDENCE as a review sequence

Express or implied claim · Verifiability · Integrity of source type · Dating and currency · Explicit attribution · Neutrality and conflicts · Confidence · Evidence disposition.

The sequence is a working evidence-operations framework created by RN Collins. It structures questions and records; it does not replace Bluebook, court, agency, publisher, journal, records-management, or domain-specific review requirements.
